How it works

Apple's developer tools, made friendly

The same location simulation Xcode uses to test apps, wrapped in a map you can click.

  1. Your Mac

    Location Jump runs a small private web page and turns your clicks into GPS coordinates.

  2. Secure tunnel

    An encrypted iOS 17 developer connection over USB or Wi-Fi, powered by pymobiledevice3.

  3. Your iPhone

    Developer Mode reports the new location to every app. Press Reset and it's back to normal.

Get started

Up and running in about ten minutes

You need a Mac (macOS 11 or later), an iPhone on iOS 17 or later, and a USB cable.

  1. 1

    Prepare your iPhone

    Set a passcode, then turn on Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode and restart. Plug it into your Mac and tap Trust.

  2. 2

    Download it

    Open Terminal and run:

    Terminal
    xcode-select --install
git clone https://github.com/IggyDuenas/location-jump.git
cd location-jump
  3. 3

    Install it once

    This sets up the secure connection (it asks for your Mac password once) and adds the app to Launchpad.

    Terminal
    ./tunnel-service.sh install
./install-app.sh
  4. 4

    Jump

    Open Location Jump from Launchpad or Spotlight. Your browser opens the map: pick a place and press Set Location.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Do I need to jailbreak my iPhone?

No. Location Jump uses Developer Mode, a setting Apple builds into every iPhone for testing apps. You can turn it off again at any time.

Does it work with Snap Map and other apps?

Yes. While a location is set, your whole phone reports it to every app, including Snapchat, Maps, Find My and the weather. Some apps take a moment to notice; reopening them helps.

How do I get my real location back?

Press Reset to Real Location or Quit. If the app isn't running anymore, simply restarting your iPhone always restores the real GPS.

Can I use it without the cable?

Yes, after the first time. With the iPhone plugged in, turn on Wi-Fi mode in the panel, then unplug. The phone and Mac need to be on the same network, and the cable stays the most reliable option.

Is it safe? What does it send where?

Nothing leaves your Mac except map tiles, place searches and street routes from OpenStreetMap. There are no accounts, no tracking and no servers. The app only accepts requests from your own Mac, and every line of code is on GitHub. The details are in the privacy policy.

Does this website collect any data?

No. There are no cookies, no analytics and no sign ups. Like any website, the services it loads from (GitHub Pages, Google Fonts and OpenStreetMap) can see your IP address. See the privacy policy.

Is it legal?

Changing your own phone's location for testing or privacy is generally fine. Using it to mislead people, commit fraud or get around location checks can break the law, and breaking an app's rules can get your account banned. You're responsible for how you use it; see the terms of use. This isn't legal advice.

Can my Snapchat or game account get banned?

It can. Many apps don't allow faking your location in their terms and can warn, limit or ban accounts that do. That's the risk you take when you use it with those apps.

Do I need a paid Apple developer account?

No. You only need to turn on Developer Mode on the iPhone, which is free and built in.

Is it safe to leave Developer Mode on?

Developer Mode lets developer tools on a computer you've trusted, like Xcode or Location Jump, control features such as location. You can turn it off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode whenever you're done.

Does it work on iPad?

Yes. iPads on iOS 17 or later work the same way as iPhones. With several devices plugged in, the app asks which one to use.

How do I update it?

In Terminal, go to the location-jump folder and run git pull, then quit and reopen Location Jump. If requirements.txt changed, also run ./tunnel-service.sh install again.

Does it work on Windows or Linux, or with Android?

Not today. Location Jump is built for macOS and for iPhones and iPads on iOS 17 or later.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. It's free and open source under the MIT license.

Use it responsibly

Location Jump is for testing apps, protecting your privacy and harmless fun with people who are in on the joke. Many apps, Snapchat included, don't allow location spoofing in their terms, and accounts can be flagged for it. Don't use it to mislead anyone or to cheat in games or services.
